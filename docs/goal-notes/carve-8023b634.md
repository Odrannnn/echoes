## The source-order trap, and the reason runs 2 and 3 both mislabelled it

The rule is unchanged and correct: **descending by retail offset** in the source
(`RUNNING_THE_DECOMP.md:2056-2058`, `tools/check_decl_order.py`'s docstring). Descending by retail
offset here means the **constructor at 0x8023B670 goes first**, because that is the *higher*
address - the destructor at 0x8023B634 is the lower one.

Both runs 2 and 3 wrote the right order and then described it as "dtor first", and run 3's notes
literally say "**constructor first** (descending by address)", which is self-contradictory. I
repeated that mistake: my first attempt put `__dt__11SLdrCommandFv` first on the reading that
0x8023B634 was "the big number". Measured, that is the *ascending* order and it is wrong:

0000000c g F .text 0000003c __dt__11SLdrCommandFv

So `carve_diff.sh` over the **whole range** is the one check that says this in seconds, and it is
worth running on *every* two-function carve: with a run of two, "descending" is a coin-flip
decided by which address has more digits. `check_decl_order.py` needs the object already at the
unit's real path, so it cannot help on the first build.
