## The one spelling that cost the run, and it is worth the next lane's time

**The destructor call must be written with compound literals at the call site. The three
function-call spellings are all wrong, and each is wrong in a different, plausible way.** Measured
this run, all with everything else in the file unchanged:

| spelling of the `fn_80004864` call | `fn_800047E0` | what changed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fn_80004864(Begin(self), End(self))`, two `static inline` helpers returning the struct | **99.90909%** (3 of 33) | home slots land at `r1+0x08/0x0C/0x10/0x14` in the **wrong pairing**: `addi r4,r1,0x10` where retail has `addi r4,r1,0x0C`, and the two `stw`s that write them swapped |
| the same, with the helpers non-`inline` | 61.757576% | the helpers stop being inlined and are emitted as `t Begin800047E0` / `t End800047E0`, so the object carries four functions and `unit_fit.sh` would reject it |
| two named locals `begin` / `end`, then `fn_80004864(begin, end)` | 90.63636% | the by-value parameters are copied straight into their home slots and the temporaries disappear; `fn_80004864` also moves to 0x80, so the unit is 4 bytes short |
| `fn_80004864((struct SIt800047E0){self->mItems}, (struct SIt800047E0){self->mItems + self->mCount})` | **100.0%** | - |

The mechanism is the one `Carve8000432C.cpp`'s header already describes for the same call, and this
is the **C** half of it: by-value class arguments are passed as pointers to caller-side
temporaries, so the frame needs two words per iterator. **The two words are allocated as a pair per
iterator, and which pair goes to which argument is decided by the expression, not by the values.**
Writing the call with compound literals makes each iterator one temporary at the call site, which is
what puts `end` in `{0x08, 0x0C}` and `begin` in `{0x10, 0x14}` as retail has it; routing them
through a named-returning helper splits the pair across the two arguments instead. This is the same
family as the `rc_ptr` r3 finding and `IsAllocValid`'s operand order recorded in
`docs/RUNNING_THE_DECOMP.md` "The carve vein": **the register a temporary lands in is a function of
how the expression is written, not of what it computes.**

Two smaller findings from the same table:

- **`static inline` is load-bearing, not decoration.** The identical body with the `inline` keyword
  removed compiles at 61.76% *and* emits the helpers as out-of-line functions; the keyword is what
  both inlines them and keeps the object to two symbols.
- **`(struct S){ .current = x }` does not compile at all** under this MWCC (`-lang=c`, GC/2.7):
  designated initialisers in a compound literal are rejected. The positional form
  `(struct SIt800047E0){self->mItems}` is the one that works.

**One accepted imperfection, measured:** the compound literals make MWCC emit an 8-byte `.sbss2`
holding the string-pool base (`nm`: `b @20`, `b @22`), and `splits.txt` claims `.text` only, so
`unit_fit.sh` prints `.sbss2 claimed - ours 8 <- NOT CLAIMED BY splits.txt` and **exits 0**. This is
the `-str reuse,pool,readonly` literal pool, not code: `main.dol`'s sha1 is
`6ef9b491d0cc08bc81a124fdedb8bfaec34d0010` with the object in the link, `matched_code 188 / 188`,
`complete_code 188 / 188`, and `flip_test` PASS. The three other spellings avoid the `.sbss2` and
none of them is byte-exact, so the trade was taken deliberately. Six objects in
`build/G2ME01/src` carry a `.sbss2`, all of them non-carve SDK units.
